01
Purpose and How to Use This Playbook

Supporting every seller with the same level of attention is an impossible task for the seller success team. The seller base is too large to invest equally across it. The decision of which sellers receive active support and which dont is therefore unavoidable. The problem is that this decision is usually made after a seller has already demonstrated commercial decline. This is too late in the sellers lifecycle because by the time poor performance is visible, the window where early support would have made a difference has already closed.

This playbook exists to bridge this gap. A machine learning model that was trained on three years of seller's data identifies which sellers are at risk of leaving in their first 60 days on the platform. This is only useful if it can be translated into action. This playbook translates the sellers risk scores from the model, segmentation matrix, and survival analysis triggers into a step by step instruction that tells the seller success team who to contact, when to act, and what to do.

The accompanying Power Bi dashboard makes those instructions operational in a live environment by giving the seller support team a daily action list, real time seller risk profiles, and trigger status for every Persuadable seller without the need to carry out any manual analysis.

Not every high risk seller is worth contacting. Ascarza (2018) demonstrates that some sellers at the top of the churn risk ranking have already decided to leave and will not respond to any intervention, while others would have stayed without any contact at all. Targeting on risk score alone wastes resources on sellers who cannot be saved and disturbs sellers who did not need help. The segmentation matrix in Section 3 directs the team toward sellers who are at risk and still reachable and not just those with the highest scores.

The playbook is organised into eight sections. Sections 2 and 3 cover the model score and introduces the segmentation matrix which tells the team which sellers to target. Section 4 covers the seven trigger thresholds and tells the team when to act across the three seller lifecycle stages. Section 5 covers how to use the dashboard day to day. Finally, Sections 6 provides recommended actions which tells the team what to do and how to communicate with the sellers.

Every seller in the dataset has a churn risk score between 0 and 1 that tells the team how likely a seller is to leave in their first 12 months of activity on the platform. A score of 0.80 means that there is an 80% probability of churn. A score of 0.20 means that there is a 20% estimated probability of churn. The score is derived from behavioural signals observed in the first 60 days of a seller's tenure calculated on three years of this platform's data.

The median churn risk score across all sellers is 0.5862. Sellers that are above this threshold are classified as high risk and sellers below it are classified as low risk. It is used as the cut off point for classifying sellers as high risk or low risk in the segmentation matrix explained in Section 3.

The score tells you how likely this seller is to churn based on their early behaviour. It does not tell you why they are at risk or whether they will respond to any outreach from the seller support team. A seller with a score of 0.86 who is still logging in to the platform daily has a different behavior to that of a seller with the same score who has not been active in the last three weeks. The trigger system covered in Section 4 and the segmentation matrix covered in Section 3 provides additional context that the score alone cannot provide.

03
The Segmentation MatrixWho to target - 3,806 sellers across 4 segments

All 3,806 sellers are assigned into four groups on two axes in the segmentation matrix. The first axis is churn risk gotten from the model score described in Section 2. The second axis is an engagement indicator that was constructed from three behavioural signals observed in the early 60 day window. These include, login count, total session minutes, and days since last platform activity. Days since last activity is inverted so that a seller who was active recently scores high and one who has been inactive for a long time scores low to ensure that higher values always mean more engaged for all three signals. These signals were standardised and averaged to make up a single composite score. Sellers above the median engagement score are classified as high engagement and sellers below it are classified as low engagement. This median split is the same approach used to classify churn risk as mentioned in section 2.

When the two axes are combined, four segments emerge. These include high risk and high engagement, high risk and low engagement, low risk and high engagement, and low risk and low engagement. Each combination makes up a quadrant with its own name, retention rate, and recommended action for the seller support team to act on.

The rationale for using this two axis approach comes from Ascarza's (2018) finding that targeting sellers mainly on churn risk wastes resources on sellers who cannot be saved and disturbs sellers who did not need help. The likelihood of a seller leaving is not what matters but whether they are engaged enough to respond to any outreach. The engagement indicator acts as a measure of how reachable a seller is. A seller who is still logging in regularly and was active on the platform recently is still reachable. A seller who hasnt logged in recently and has not been active on the platform recently, can be assumed to be unreachable.

04
The Trigger SystemWhen to act - 7 triggers across 3 lifecycle stages

The trigger system tells the seller support team when to carry out intervention measures. A seller's churn risk does not remain the same all through their tenure on the platform. It increases at specific moments such as when their momentum in the early period reduces or when activity suddenly drops after a period of consistent engagement. Survival analysis, which is a method that tells you how long sellers remain active and at what point the risk of exit is highest, confirms that sellers are more likely to exit at certain points in their tenure than others rather than dropping off at a consistent rate throughout (Fader et al., 2018; Kimitei et al., 2025). This method was used to identify seven triggers across three lifecycle stages, with each being derived from this platform's own three years of seller data rather than external benchmarks. Every retention rate figure below compares sellers who crossed a threshold against those who did not. The trigger system applies to the Persuadable sellers group to alert the support team on when to take action on sellers within the group.

Early TenureDays 0 to 90 | 4 triggers | Highest risk period

The first 90 days are the highest risk period in a seller's lifecycle. In this period, sellers have not yet developed assets such as reviews or buyer relationships to keep them on the platform if thing go wrong. The switching cost that would normally make leaving difficult has not yet developed. The survival analysis shows that hazard rate increases sharply between months three and four at 17.1% which means that early intervention has a large chance of changing the outcome in the period leading up to that sharp increase. There are four triggers that apply at this stage.

A seller who generated some activity in their first month but shows reduced activity in their second month is showing early signs of failing before any real commercial momentum is gained. The most likely cause is that they have listings that are not converting due to issues such as too few images or their listings are overpriced compared to competitors. The recommended response by the seller support team is to carry out a listing quality review by checking images and pricing against category benchmarks.

A seller with disputes in their first 60 days has a retention rate of 15.4% compared to 32.2% for sellers who exceed this threshold. A dispute at this stage is a problem because it comes before the seller has built up a performance history or reviews that can balance it out. New sellers dont necessarily know how to handle the platforms dispute process effectively, and without support they are likely to disengage instead of working through it. An unresolved dispute in the early period can damage the seller's score and reduce listing visibility. The recommended response by the seller support team is to carry out immediate dispute resolution support and make direct contact with the seller within 24 hours.

Mid TenureDays 90 to 270 | 3 triggers | Engagement continuity

There are three triggers that apply in the mid tenure stage of the sellers lifecycle. A seller that has reached 90 days on the platform has passed that initial risk window. The assumption is that they have done enough to establish themselves on the platform thereby changing the focus from whether they are engaging to whether they are maintaining that engagement consistently. A seller who regularly logged into the platform for three months and then goes quiet is showing a different pattern to a seller who was never engaged in the first place as seen in the early tenure. The mid tenure triggers are designed to identify these type of patterns in seller behavior.

A seller who has been inactive for 7 or more days has a retention rate of 19.5% compared to 36.9% for sellers who exceed this threshold. Seven days of inactivity in the mid tenure is not necessarily alarming because a seller could be absent for a week due to situations such as travel which are unrelated to their intention to leave. This trigger is valuable because it gives the team an early opportunity to check in before the situation develops further. The recommended response by the seller support team is to send a re-engagement message to check in on the seller's experience on the platform.
Response: Re-engagement message - a brief check-in on the seller's experience. Light touch, not an escalation.

A seller who has been inactive for 14 or more days has a retention rate of 12.9% compared to 34.7% for sellers who exceed this threshold. A seller who has gone two weeks without logging in or processing orders can be said to have churned. The research on disengagement patterns shows that sellers do not usually leave all of a sudden. They gradually withdraw by first reducing how often they login, then they stop updating their listings and then they become completely quiet before formally exiting. At 14 days without activity, they are likely already in that withdrawal process. The longer the team waits to make contact, the harder it becomes to reverse the disengagement. A seller with a logistics fault rate above 0.124 has a retention rate of 20.2% compared to 41.2% for sellers who exceed this threshold. This trigger needs to be carefully handled because in the Nigerian operating environment, a logistics failure is not always the seller's fault. Poor roads, unreliable courier networks, and infrastructure gaps outside Lagos can lead to faults that have nothing to do with how the seller is running their business. When this trigger becomes active, the first step is to understand the source of the failures before taking action. The recommended response by the seller support team is a diagnostic review of why the fault happened and not a performance warning.
Response: Diagnostic review - understand the source of failures before acting. Not a performance warning.
Established TenureDays 270 and above | 2 triggers

There are two triggers that apply in the established tenure stage of the sellers lifecycle.

An established seller who has been consistently active for nine months or more and then suddenly goes quiet for two weeks is showing a much more unusual pattern than a mid tenure seller going inactive for the same period. The difference between their prior behaviour and their current absence is a signal that something has changed. The recommended response by the seller support team is to urgently contact the seller.

A logistics fault rate above 0.124 should be responded to with the same diagnostic approach as mid tenure. At this stage, however, a seller who has had frequent faults despite prior platform experience needs a deeper conversation about whether operational support would help rather than a standard logistics review.

When Multiple Triggers Fire Simultaneously

When a Persuadable seller has crossed more than one trigger, the following take priority.

1stDispute - The retention differential is the largest of any trigger and the risk of immediate exit is highest. Contact within 24 hours regardless of what other triggers are also active.
2ndExtended Inactivity - A seller who is both inactive and has operational problems is at greater risk of exit than when either signal is considered alone.
3rdHigh Logistics Fault Rate - It is the trigger most likely to have a cause outside the seller's control.

Daily Workflow with the DashboardCommand Centre, Watchlist, Triggers, Drill-Down

The Power BI Dashboard is the operational platform for this playbook. It has four pages with each designed for a specific purpose. The workflow below describes how to move through them as part of a daily routine. Every piece of information needed to act on this playbook is available in the dashboard.

Before navigating the dashboard, it is important to understand the traffic light system that runs through it. Every Persuadable seller is assigned one of four statuses based on their trigger profile. Red means that a dispute trigger is active or at least two triggers are active. Amber means that exactly one non-dispute trigger is active. Green means that no triggers have been crossed and Grey means that the seller is not a Persuadable and no action is required regardless of how many triggers show. These four statuses appear throughout the different pages of the dashboard and they guide the team as they work through their daily action list.

The Persuadables watchlist shows all 513 Persuadable sellers with their current trigger status, churn risk score, tenure stage, and recommended intervention. 370 of the 513 Persuadables have crossed at least one trigger and require active action. These 370 are the sellers the team must prioritize to ensure they do not churn.

Three filter buttons at the top of the page allow the team to narrow the watchlist by lifecycle stage. 64 of the 513 Persuadable sellers are in the early tenure, 322 in the mid tenure, and 127 in the established tenure. Of the 370 that require action by the team, 48 of them are early tenure sellers, 230 are mid tenure sellers, and 92 are established sellers. This provides the team with all of the information they need to decide on what to do next for each seller. Step 3 - The Tenure Triggers PagePeriodic use - population-level trigger patterns

This page shows how many sellers in the population have crossed each trigger threshold within a given lifecycle stage. It is designed to be used periodically to understand where exit risk is currently concentrating across the platform. There are three tabs that switch between Early, Mid, and Established tenure views. Each of them shows how many sellers have crossed each trigger within that stage. It allows the team to spot patterns that are worth investigating across each trigger for the entire population.

Step 4 - Individual Seller Drill-DownUse before every seller contact - full seller profile

The individual seller drill-down page provides the full picture for a single seller in one view and should be the last step before contacting a seller.

The top row shows the seller's segment, tenure stage, and registration cohort. Below it, three cards show the churn risk score and retention probability, the current alert status, and the number of triggers crossed out of six possible. Six trigger flag icons show which specific thresholds are active for this seller.

The Behavioural Metrics panel shows eight metrics from the seller's 60 day observation window alongside the population average. These metrics include logins, session minutes, days since last activity, total deliveries, second half orders, disputes, logistics fault rate, and average seller score. Any metric that is very different from the population average is highlighted in red. This table allows the team understand what factors drive the seller's risk score before making contact.

The Order Trajectory panel shows the number of orders for the seller in the first 30 days versus the second 30 days of their observation window with the percentage change. A seller who received 9 orders in their first 30 days and only 6 in their second for example, shows a 30% decrease in order volume before they have even reached the end of their observation window. That downward movement in the early weeks of tenure is a clear signal that a seller's business on the platform is slowing down rather than growing.

The Seller Profile panel shows the seller's state, LGA, referral source, verification tier, and whether they operate in a category that depends on imported goods. This context matters especially when a high logistics fault rate trigger becomes active, since sellers in some states outside Lagos face infrastructure constraints that leads to faults which are not related to the sellers behaviour.

At the bottom of the page, the Recommended Action field states the specific intervention the seller support team should take. Section 6 provides the full step by step response for each scenario the team is likely to encounter.

Recommended Actions by Scenario6 step-by-step response guides for the seller support team

This section translates the triggers from Section 4 into steps for the six most common situations the seller success team will encounter. Each scenario describes the sellers profile, what signal prompted the action taken, and exactly what to do. Before contacting any seller, a seller support team member should open the Individual Seller Drill-Down in the dashboard and review the sellers full profile. The recommended action field at the bottom of that page will confirm which scenario applies.

1New Seller - No Engagement Signal by Day 30

The seller registered within the last 30 days and has less than 3 logins with very little session time. The seller has not placed any order yet. No trigger would be active yet but the early pattern suggests that the low login and low session thresholds will be crossed before day 60 if nothing changes.

1Open the seller's Individual Drill-Down and review their category and registration source before making any contact.
2Send a personalised message welcoming them to the platform and offering a resource that is relevant to their product category, such as a listing guide or a benchmark for prices in their category.
3If there is no response or there hasnt been an increase in login activity on the platform within five days, then the action should be to escalate to a direct call.
2New Seller - First Dispute Received - Red

The seller is in the early tenure and a dispute has been opened against them. This scenario makes the dispute trigger active and it makes the seller's status Red.

1The team should contact the seller within 24 hours. The 24 hour window is important because a dispute that is left unaddressed in early tenure worsens quickly and the seller's score and listing visibility can both be affected before the team has had a chance to help.
2Before contacting the seller, the support team member should open the Individual Drill-Down and check the logistics fault rate and delivery zone. If the seller is based outside Lagos in an area where there are known infrastructure challenges, the dispute may be logistics related rather than seller related. This changes how the conversation with the seller should go.
3When contact is made with the seller, the support person should ask for a description of what happened before offering any guidance. By listening first, trust is established and it also provides the information needed to advise them accurately.
4The next step would be to walk the seller through the dispute resolution process step by step as they may not know how it works. New sellers usually disengage from disputes because the process may be unfamiliar to them.
5The seller support team member should then follow up within 48 hours to confirm the dispute has been responded to.
3New Seller - Low Session Time and Low Second-Month Orders - Red

The seller is in early tenure and both the low session trigger and the low orders in days 30 to 60 trigger have become active at the same time making the seller's status Red.

1Open the Individual Drill-Down and check the order trajectory panel to confirm that orders declined from the first half to the second half of the observation window.
2Review the seller's active listings to check if there are quality issues like having lower than three images, having a title under six words, or pricing listings far above the category average.
3The seller should only be contacted when there is a specific observation and a potential solution rather than just a general check in.
4If there is no improvement in login activity within 10 days, this should be flagged for escalation.
4Mid-Tenure Seller - Sudden Inactivity After Active Period - Red

When a seller has been active for between 90 and 270 days with a consistent prior engagement history and suddenly becomes inactive the 14 day inactivity trigger becomes active with the seller's status set to Red.

1Before contacting the seller, the support team member should open the Individual Drill-Down and review the order trajectory and logistics fault rate. They should check whether the inactivity happened after there was a dispute or an increase in fault rate. This would suggest that there is a specific cause for the inactivity rather than a gradual withdrawal.
2The next step is for the team to make direct contact with the seller by phone where possible. A seller who was previously active and has suddenly gone quiet is more likely to respond to a personal call than a written message.
3The support team member should open the conversation by enquiring about their experience on the platform and asking about what has been happening with their business recently rather than referring to their absence directly. The reason for the inactivity may not be related to the platform and the team's role is to diagnose before recommending anything.
4Based on their response, the seller should be directed to the most relevant support resource.
5Mid-Tenure Seller - High Logistics Fault Rate - Amber or Red

When a sellers logistics fault rate has crossed 0.124, the high fault rate trigger becomes active with the sellers status set to Amber or Red depending on whether any other trigger is also active.

1Before contacting the seller, the support team member should open the Individual Drill-Down and check the seller's state and LGA. A seller based outside Lagos in an area where there are infrastructure challenges should be approached with the aim of diagnosing the problem and not treated immediately as a performance issue.
2The team member should then check the order trajectory panel to confirm if there is a high fault rate alongside a declining order trajectory. A combination of both is a bigger problem than just a high fault rate with stable order volume.
3The seller should then be contacted and asked if they have noticed any pattern where orders have been failing to be delivered. Let them describe the situation before drawing any conclusions.
4If the faults appear to be related to a courier service, the team should connect the seller with the platform's preferred logistics partner for their delivery zone.
5If the faults appear to be from the sellers side, such as incorrect addresses or packaging problems, the team should address the specific issue directly.
6It is important that when the seller is contacted, it should not be framed as a warning or a performance review. The goal is to identify the source of the problem and help the seller fix it.
6Persuadable Seller - No Triggers Active - Green

A seller that is classified as Persuadable based on their churn risk score and engagement indicator, but no trigger threshold has been crossed will have a Green status.

1This seller does not require immediate action because a Green status means that the churn risk is high but no specific trigger has been crossed yet.
2The team member should check the Individual Drill-Down once a week to check whether any trigger is approaching its threshold. They should pay close attention to the login trend and order trajectory panels.
3If the sellers churn risk score is above 0.80 and the order trajectory starts reducing, the team should consider checking in on the seller even without any trigger being crossed. This should be framed as a form of support rather than concern.
4This seller should not be treated with the same urgency as a Red or Amber seller. Treating issues with this seller with any urgency wastes time the team should be spending on sellers who need it more.

ConclusionMaintenance, measurement and improvement
This playbook exists because a model score on its own does not change anything for a seller who is at risk of leaving. The model identifies which sellers are at risk and which are still reachable and the playbook tells the team what to do with that information.

The dashboard is an operational tool for the seller support team to manage the at risk sellers on a day to day basis.

The most important thing the team need to keep in mind is prioritisation because not every seller needs their attention and not every high risk seller will respond to an outreach. The segmentation matrix exists to solve this problem by directing time and resources towards sellers who are at risk and still active on the platform. The Persuadables Watchlist ensures the sellers that are most likely to exit without an intervention get contacted first.

Maintaining the Playbook

The triggers and segments used in this playbook were derived from three years of the platform's seller data. They reflect the conditions of selling in the Nigerian e-commerce environment that are unique to this context. These conditions however are not permanent. As the platform grows and operating conditions change, the model would need to be recalibrated with all identified thresholds reviewed. A model that was trained on one period's data will change as seller behaviour patterns evolve and retention rates shift. All thresholds that were derived from historical data will therefore lose their accuracy if the population changes. The playbook would need to be updated to reflect any changes if they occur.

Measuring Effectiveness

To measure if this playbook is working, the team must track if Persuadable sellers who received intervention have a higher retention rate than those who did not. Data that shows the trigger that led to a seller to be contacted and whether the seller is still active 90 days after should be collected daily. This provides the information necessary to improve this playbook in the future.
